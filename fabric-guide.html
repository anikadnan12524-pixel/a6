The fabric guide

Twelve common fabrics explained: how they feel, how they wear, what they’re best for and how to look after them.

Natural, semi-synthetic and synthetic

Know your fibres

Fabrics fall into three families. Natural fibres come from plants and animals, semi-synthetics are made from processed plant material such as wood pulp, and synthetics are made from petroleum-based polymers. Each has strengths, and blends often combine them.

CottonNatural

Soft, breathable and easy to wash; can shrink and crease.

Great for
T-shirts, shirts, jeans, underwear
Care
Wash at 30–40°C; tumble low or line dry

LinenNatural

Very cool and strong; creases easily and softens with age.

Great for
Summer shirts, trousers, dresses
Care
Wash at 30–40°C; iron damp

WoolNatural

Warm, breathable and odour-resistant; can feel itchy if coarse.

Great for
Knitwear, coats, suits, socks
Care
Air often; hand or wool wash; dry flat

Merino woolNatural

Fine, soft wool that regulates temperature well.

Great for
Base layers, fine knits, travel wear
Care
Wool cycle at 30°C; dry flat

SilkNatural

Smooth, light and lustrous; delicate and can water-mark.

Great for
Blouses, scarves, dresses, linings
Care
Hand wash cool or dry clean

DenimCotton twill

Durable cotton twill that moulds to you over time.

Great for
Jeans, jackets, skirts
Care
Wash rarely, cold and inside out

Viscose (rayon)Semi-synthetic

Drapes beautifully and feels cool; weak when wet.

Great for
Dresses, blouses, linings
Care
Gentle wash at 30°C; line dry

LyocellSemi-synthetic

Soft, smooth and breathable, made in a closed-loop process.

Great for
Shirts, dresses, bedding-weight fabrics
Care
Gentle wash at 30°C

PolyesterSynthetic

Strong, quick-drying and crease-resistant; less breathable.

Great for
Activewear, outerwear, blends
Care
Wash at 30–40°C; cool iron

NylonSynthetic

Light, strong and stretchy; dries quickly.

Great for
Tights, swimwear, rain jackets
Care
Cool wash; no high heat

Elastane (spandex)Synthetic

Added in small amounts for stretch and recovery.

Great for
Jeans, leggings, fitted tops
Care
Avoid hot washes and dryers

FleeceSynthetic

Warm, light and quick-drying; can pill.

Great for
Mid-layers, jackets, loungewear
Care
Cool wash inside out; skip softener
Weaves and knits

Why the same fibre can feel so different

  • Poplin: a tight, smooth plain weave used for crisp shirts.
  • Oxford: a basket weave with texture; relaxed and durable.
  • Twill: diagonal lines, as in denim and chinos; hides marks and drapes well.
  • Jersey: a single knit that stretches; the fabric of T-shirts.
  • Rib: stretchy vertical ridges for cuffs, collars and fitted tops.
Spotting quality

Signs of a well-made garment

  • Patterns match at the seams.
  • Stitches are small, even and secure.
  • Seams are finished neatly inside.
  • Buttons are sewn on firmly, ideally with a spare.
  • The fabric recovers when you gently stretch it.

Basic sewing skills, like replacing a button or re-stitching a seam, are the easiest way to keep good clothes in use for years.
