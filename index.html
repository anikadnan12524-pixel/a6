    Clothing basics

    Six things everyone should know about clothes

    01

    Fit first

    Shoulders and length are hardest to alter, so get those right when you buy.

    02

    Read the label

    Fibre content tells you how a piece will feel, wear and wash.

    03

    Check the seams

    Even, tight stitching and generous seam allowances signal quality.

    04

    Wash cool

    30°C (86°F) cleans most clothes and helps them last longer.

    05

    Know your measurements

    Chest, waist, hips and inside leg make online shopping much easier.

    06

    Mend early

    A loose button or small hole is quick to fix before it gets worse.

    Shopping checklist

    Before you buy anything new

    • Check your size on the brand’s chart, not just the label you usually wear.
    • Feel the fabric and read the fibre content.
    • Look at the care label: will you really hand wash it?
    • Picture three outfits with clothes you already own.
    • Check the return policy when shopping online.

    Caring for clothes

    Make your clothes last

    • Air before washing: many clothes don’t need a wash after one wear.
    • Turn dark and printed clothes inside out.
    • Line-dry where you can to save fibres and energy.
    • Fold knits, hang wovens.
    • Store off-season clothes clean and dry.

    FAQ

    Clothing questions, answered

    How do I convert my clothing size between countries?

    Use our size converter for tops, dresses, trousers and shoes. Sizes are approximate, as brands cut differently, so always check the brand’s own chart and your body measurements.

    What is the most breathable fabric for hot weather?

    Linen is the classic choice, followed by lightweight cotton, cotton voile and seersucker. Loose weaves and relaxed fits let air move, which matters as much as the fibre.

    Why do my clothes shrink in the wash?

    Natural fibres such as cotton and wool contract with heat and agitation. Wash at 30°C (86°F), skip the tumble dryer for anything you care about and reshape knits while damp.

    How do I measure myself for clothes?

    Use a soft tape measure over light clothing. Measure the fullest part of the chest or bust, the natural waist and the fullest part of the hips, keeping the tape level and snug but not tight.

    What should I do with clothes I no longer wear?

    Repair what’s worth saving, pass good-condition pieces to friends or charity, and take worn-out textiles to a textile recycling point rather than the bin.

    Is natural fabric always better than synthetic?

    Not always. Natural fibres breathe well and age gracefully, while synthetics such as nylon and polyester can be more durable, quick-drying and stretchy. Blends often combine the best of both.
